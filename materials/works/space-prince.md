# Space Prince

*2026, in progress · Digital game*

*Space Prince* is a game you play as a birth chart.
You give a time and a place, and the game works out where the Sun, the Moon, and five planets stood in the sky at that moment.
That chart is your character sheet, your game board, and your save file.

A run is a series of encounters.
When your chart meets another, you choose which of your planets to send and how it should act, and the effect travels along the lines connecting the planets in the other chart.
Responding well earns Light, the game’s score.
When the run ends, the Light you have left becomes a star in your sky.
Every run leaves one, bright or dim.

The idea arrived at a gallery opening in January 2026.
The director of Artist Commons told me the program started that Monday and asked what I would make.
I had been admiring *Loot Survivor 2*, which builds a rich dungeon out of rock-paper-scissors rules because it has to fit on a blockchain.
My last two games had treated the save file as the artwork.
A birth chart struck me as a natural save file, one that is already personal before you make a single move.

You can play as anyone, but I care most about playing as yourself.
I respect astrology as a symbolic system, and I want the rules to follow it closely enough that playing teaches you to read a chart.
If you later had your own chart read, you would recognize parts of it from experience.
Most people know their sign.
I would like a player to come away thinking instead: I have a Sun, I have a Moon, I have a Mars.
That shift matters to me.
“I am an Aquarius” once gave me a pleasant, mythic idea of myself.
“I am made of planets and drives” leaves room to notice that some of what I do helps other people and some of it is careless.

Hermann Hesse’s *Siddhartha* is my model for the play I am after.
Its prince tries one life after another, finds enlightenment in none of them, and is changed by all of them.
I want an encounter to reward what I try to practice in my own life: responding instead of reacting, with skill and grace.
The game does not do this well enough yet, and it is the main thing I am still working on.
I would like a player to be able to point to a star and remember a situation they handled well.
A dim star from a run that went badly is a memory too.

The look and the sound are further along.
I wanted the chart to have the finish of jewelry and still be easy to read as a game board, and working through that tension produced its present form.
The music follows a rule of its own.
Game music tends to stay in one key so that tracks can follow one another in any order.
I still wanted each planet to have its own theme, as in Holst’s *The Planets*.
So every theme is in D, and each of the seven planets takes a different one of the seven musical modes.
I worked with an AI model to generate the tracks from that scheme and each planet’s symbolism, then swapped out the sounds that grated on my ear.

Of my earlier works, the one I would show beside it is *Identity Matrix*.
The curiosity is the same: how much beauty a system of color and form can produce.
