# The Lost Temple Adventure

*2025 · Onchain game and in-person treasure hunt · Devconnect, Buenos Aires*

Some of my old teammates from Colony used to put on elaborate scavenger hunts at Ethereum conferences, and I’d helped with a few over the years.
When I heard that the friends behind Daedalus Industries and Social Dist0rtion Protocol were sitting out Devconnect in Buenos Aires, I asked if they’d mind my pitching a hunt through Cartridge.
With *Ronin’s Pact* I’d tried making a digital badge that recorded what you’d done.
Now I had a whole conference venue to play with.

I put up little flyers asking, “Can you find the lost temple?”
The lost temple was about as much plot as I thought we needed.
I wanted someone passing a flyer to have just enough of a story to pull out their phone and set off.
They’d get a map, play games on their phone, and follow clues to physical puzzles around the venue.

The map was the save file and the artwork you’d get to keep.
I’d been thinking about Erick Calderon’s idea of a “1 of 1 of X.”
I could use the same small program to give every player a different arrangement of waypoints, then reveal their route as they went.
Working out how to get that variety within the limits of an onchain program reminded me of laying out the nine stencils in *Identity Matrix*.
What I wanted to find out here was whether somebody might come to care about their particular map because of the afternoon they’d spent filling it in.

For one puzzle I put out a Super Nintendo, some Konami games, and back issues of *Nintendo Power*.
I’d hoped to find an old television locally and hook up the console.
In the end the Nintendo sat there as a sculptural object.
The puzzle was called *Cracking the Code*, and I was sure people would put the clues together and think of the Konami Code.
As I remember it, everybody needed a hint.
Fortunately a friend had talked me into putting a Telegram help link in the game.
I spent a good part of the conference answering people and giving them hints so they could keep going.

The photographs I’d like to show from that week are the ones of people doing the puzzles together.
