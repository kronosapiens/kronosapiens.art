# Artist website

Start with who is visiting and what they came to decide.
A curator considering a game for a show may need to see what playing it looks like, then find a way to reach Daniel.
Someone who played it may come back for the map they remember.
Use Daniel’s stated audience and purpose when available.
If the purpose is still open, show which choice depends on it.

## Choose and order the work

- Choose the opening work or pair that best shows what Daniel makes today.
  For each additional work, ask what a visitor would lose if it came out.
  An older mural can earn its place by making a rule visible that is harder to see in a game.
- Keep a project’s images together when someone needs several views to grasp it.
  Place works from different years or media together when seeing one changes what a viewer can notice in the other.
  Choose the closing work for the impression it leaves of where the practice is going.
- Let the homepage make a selection and the Works index support browsing.
  On this site, `lib/pictures.ts` sets the opening pair and `materials/works.md` supplies the index order and blurbs.
  Revisit what deserves the opening when a new work arrives.
- Read the homepage with the opening images in view.
  It shows just the first sentence of `materials/statement.md`, so the pairing and its labels do much of the explaining.
  About gives an interested visitor the full statement.

## Choose what the visitor sees

- Start with a legible view of the work and check what a visitor can still make out at its displayed size on a phone.
  For a mural, look for a straight-on photograph that preserves its colors and proportions.
  Use a detail to reveal something small, an installation view to establish scale or place, or a sequence to show what changes through participation.
- For *The Lost Temple Adventure*, the animated map shows what solving a puzzle adds to the player’s keepsake.
  The photograph of players at the Jenga table answers a different question about what happened in the venue.
  Choose the lead according to which aspect the visitor needs to grasp first.
- Give the work a readable title, year and specific medium.
  Include physical dimensions when known and useful, and credit collaborators where their contribution appears.
  Identify a sample rendering, enhancement or work in progress when that distinction changes what the image establishes.
  In `lib/pictures.ts`, the first image for a work leads on its page and represents it on the homepage and Works index.
  Check all three placements when proposing a change.

## Review a visit

Try two entrances, the homepage and a link straight to a work.
From each, can someone identify what they are seeing, find evidence for the page’s main claim, and follow their interest?
Trace the next step that matters for this visitor, whether that is seeing a related work, watching play, or making an inquiry.
Report where the path succeeds or breaks, with the page, image or missing information that accounts for it.
